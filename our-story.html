We build clothes the way our grandparents did.

Slowly, deliberately, and with the expectation that they'd be worn for years — then handed down, not thrown out.

How it started

KLO began with a jacket that wouldn't fall apart.

It wasn't a business plan. It was a beat-up field jacket, inherited, patched twice, and still in daily rotation after fifteen years — while everything bought new that same decade had long since worn out, gone out of style, or ended up in a landfill.

That contrast became the whole premise of KLO: build fewer garments, build them properly, and stand behind them for as long as someone chooses to wear them. Keep. Live. Old. Not a slogan — an instruction.

We started with three styles cut from deadstock fabric in a single workshop. We've grown since, but the instruction hasn't changed.

Where we've been

Small runs, deliberate growth.

2019

Three styles, one workshop

KLO launched with a field jacket, a raw denim, and a heavyweight tee — all cut from deadstock fabric to avoid producing anything new from scratch.

2021

The repair promise

We committed to free mending on every KLO garment for as long as it's owned — turning "returns" into "repairs" as the default relationship with a worn item.

2023

Men's and Women's lines

We expanded from a single unisex capsule into dedicated Men's and Women's collections, without changing how each piece was built.

2026

Archive 04

Our latest collection — still made in small, produced-to-order batches, still repaired for free, still built to be worn a decade from now.

What we stand for

The instruction behind every garment.

Four commitments that shape every decision, from fabric sourcing to how we handle a return.

01

Built to last

Overbuilt seams, natural fibers, and cuts stress-tested for years of wear — not one season of trend cycles.

02

Built to mend

Every piece ships with a repair kit, and we mend it for free for as long as you own it. No fine print.

03

Made in small runs

We produce to order in limited batches. No warehouses of unsold stock, no overproduction to write off.

04

Honest about cost

Our prices reflect what it actually costs to make something well — and pay the people who make it fairly.
